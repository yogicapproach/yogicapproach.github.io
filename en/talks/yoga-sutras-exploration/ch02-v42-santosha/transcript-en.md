## Opening

*Hari Om Tat Sat* to one and all. Many familiar faces. I hope this finds you well.

My gratitude to Janardhana and the Instituto Yoga Carrasco for the continuation of these series, and for me being a guest on it.

It had been some years since I had really visited the *yoga sutras* in an intentional way. It has really brought me a lot of joy, but also deep reflection that affects my daily life, by putting my mind back into these *sutras*.

I think we all have experienced that to be a recipient to someone else talking is one type of experience. And to revisit that same terrain on your own can really be a very different experience. One of the intentions of these sessions is to inspire and motivate you to look into the *sutras* in your own way. To experiment with them. To take your favourite yogic topic or greatest life engagement or challenge. And for any *sutra* to ask: is this true? Have I experienced it for myself, or can I get to an experience of this *sutra*? Otherwise, there will always be a separation of words, experience and technique.

For many of our sessions I have found it natural to look through the lens of the *koshas*, because I'm really in love with them these past two years. In short: find a way to develop a relationship with each of these powerful phrases.

We will start with *Shantipath* — and pardon me for any noise that may come in today, there's construction going on.

## Settling into Contentment

> *PRACTICE — Settling: finding a position you can be content in (body, breath, mind), then Shantipath*

Bring yourself into a comfortable position. One that you imagine, or actually feel, you can remain content in. An arrangement where there comes a moment where you don't feel you need to improve in some way. An arrangement where you can relax into yourself.

Do what you can with the body to help facilitate this. Do what you can with your hands to help facilitate this — maybe put them in a *mudra*. Do what you can with your face, your lips, your tongue and eyes to support this sense of contentment. If you're ever unsure, see if you can sit with that not knowing, and just be content to be in that state.

Similarly, for the breath: be the witness, and recognise if you are giving yourself instructions to breathe a certain way, or if there is even an awareness of the breath flowing in and out without judgment. And find a way to breathe that's between effort and giving up.

And finally, bring the awareness to the mental space. Noting which senses are active or taking in input. Noticing if memory is active or quiet. If the intellect is chewing, or digesting. Then give it an object — an object of luminosity to gaze upon.

You imagine yourself sitting inside your head, and on a table is a steady golden flame at the front, near the forehead. This flame has a unique quality: it is at peace with its being. It has enough fuel to continue burning. It's protected on all sides from wind and water. So it continues to do what it was brought to do, which is to give off light.

With our awareness on that inner luminosity, we will chant the mantra *OM* three times together, and the *Shanti* mantras together as well. Take a slow, full breath in to chant the mantra *OM*.

> *CHANT — OM ×3, followed by the Shanti mantras*

You can bring the hands into *pranam mudra*, the prayer position, touching the heart. Take a deep breath in and out, feeling the torso expand and contract. Then move the hands into a vigorous round of palming, with the intention of creating heat. Then cover the eyes with the palms; reabsorb. After one more deep inhalation, relax the hands down with the breath out.

## The Sutra

We continue this journey of qualities that we are cultivating within ourselves. The last session was cleanliness, or *shaucha*. And today it is *santosha*.

The verse itself is quite short. *Santoshat paramam labham* — by contentment you will have great gain. Let me reread it in Swami Satyananda's own words: **unexcelled happiness comes from the practice of contentment.**

I love, anytime we have Sanskrit phrases, to look at one or two of the words, so that we begin to learn common prefixes and roots. We'll do that in a moment. But the word to take note of is *unexcelled* — unsurpassable, the highest, total.

Because his commentary on this *sutra* is quite short, I'd like to play a one minute and eight second translation of it, which hopefully came out correct in Spanish.

## Where Do I Start From?

With any of these invitations to cultivate such high qualities, the question is always: where do I start from? The techniques we have through the various branches of yoga are, I believe, designed for the rainbow shades of personality that we are.

I want to highlight just one more commentary, from Veda Vyasa. He simply says: by contentment, one acquires extreme happiness. In his view, if one can continue to watch how desires never dissipate, it leads to a state of not wanting to chase them. That requires us to develop the ability to be a constant witness of our own lives.

Similarly, what I have experienced with Swami Satyananda and Satyananda Yoga is that awareness is the foundation for all personal growth and evolution.

This point he made about dissatisfaction with oneself is particularly relevant to many of us. When we think about our body — would we edit it if we could, and how so? When we think about our capacity to do work or achieve our goals — is there a feeling of enough, or a narrative of not enough, more, need to achieve more? Certainly in the mental dimension as well: am I smart enough, well spoken, well read? In the emotional dimension: do I know how to relate to others? Are my relationships harmonious?

Can *santosha* actually be practised? And if so, what are some avenues to move into that state, even if it's only for a moment, to have a taste of it? In this regard, a spiritual diary is invaluable.

## The Word: *san-*

Before we get into some simple practices, I want to go over the breakdown of the word *santosha*.

The prefix *san*, you will come across in many, many yogic words: *santosha*, *sannyasa*, *samsara*, *sankalpa*. In all of these cases, *san* will mean fully, completely, totally.

In this case, *san* plus *tush* is total satisfaction. Nothing lacking. We translate that as contentment.

*Sannyasa* — total discard, total throwing. We translate that as renunciation. *Sankalpa* — total gathering, total determination. We translate that as an intention, or a resolve.

When you think about the word *santosha*, I would love for you to develop a feeling behind it. Not a meaning, but a feeling. That's the state we will try to connect with in a few different positions.

What does it really mean to be totally satisfied, to be totally content, in any physical position? In any rhythm the breath has chosen. In any wave or *vritti* that the mind is now taking.

## Makarasana — Contentment in the Body

> *PRACTICE — Makarasana (crocodile pose), awareness in Annamaya kosha*

We start in *makarasana*, the crocodile pose, on the stomach. Check that you have proper padding below you, so that there is a starting point of general comfort. The heels can roll outwards as comfortable, the forearms perpendicular with the floor. The total weight of the head and neck resting on the palms below the chin. Adjust the elbows forwards, away from the sternum or towards the sternum, such that you feel a uniform curve in the spine. Once you have found a position of symmetry and stillness, hold it.

We spend the first stage with our awareness in the whole of the *Annamaya kosha*.

To engage with the reflection of *how do I feel*, there must be the witness and the observer. What am I feeling right now, anywhere in the body? Where am I feeling it? What is the intensity of that sensation or experience? And do I have an intellectual or emotional response to that sensation or experience?

Do not feel the need to unpack that last question if it is a yes — only to recognise if there is an opinion to what is taking place within.

In particular, if you feel any discomfort or dis-ease, rather than adjusting immediately, see if you can be present with it for any length of time.

In this way of approaching *hatha yoga*, we give ourselves very simple arrangements to find contentment, or *santosha*, in. Then gradually, intentionally, create far more challenging physical positions.

What state of mind do you have to shift to, if you were going to hold this position for two hours? When you begin to imagine that state of long-term acceptance of the current position, what shifts internally to give space for that?

At any time, if the body demands adjustment, ask yourself: does it really require it? Is it really needed? And if it is, make it mindfully. If not, see if you can sit with it for some time longer.

## The Breath — Natural, Deepening, Spinal

> *PRACTICE — breath awareness in makarasana, into spinal breathing with the chakra names*

Now shift the awareness to the breath. Initially, simply observing the natural breath. Anytime you observe the focus moving away from observation of the breath, recognising that, gently turn it back towards that.

Without managing the natural breath in any way, begin to follow it into yourself as deeply as you can sense it, and to follow it back out until it leaves the nasal passages.

With each new breath, begin to make it slightly more intentional, slightly deeper. The head of the breath moving all the way down to the pelvic floor and expanding the pelvic region, then the abdomen, then the chest, then the shoulders, up to the neck. Always working with the breath according to its capacity, versus your imagination of your own capacity.

The attitude is similar to working with a child: you're not trying to shape them into anything, but to give them space to move into their full potential. And the breath is a child. It is ever youthful. It's what gives us our vitality and youthfulness. The bones are old. The skin is old. But the breath is always fresh, always young — so we relate to it as such.

Show the pathway of the spine to the breath, and escort it from tailbone up to back of head, inhaling; back of head, back down the spine to tailbone, exhaling.

On this journey up and down the spine, maybe you don't make it all the way up — and see how you feel about that. Can you move into spinal breathing with a sense of acceptance of how you are today, that there is no goal, just a path to move along?

And finally, for about the next minute, if you know the names of the *chakras*, say them as the breath passes through that point in both directions.

## Shavasana — Seeing Yourself as You Are

> *PRACTICE — Shavasana: body, energy and mind in this stage of life*

Mentally prepare to come out of this position. You're going to come out, and then come onto your back into *shavasana*. Begin that transition when ready. Physical symmetry, balance, and checking that every part of you is well supported.

Awareness of the whole body, as if there is a mirror on the ceiling and you're looking back down at your own reflection. For a few more seconds, total awareness of the body — and really seeing yourself, your body, as it is in this life stage that you're in.

A few more seconds of reflection on your energetic state, and how you generally have been feeling these months and year.

A few moments of reflection on your mental state, your mental condition, in this year of your life, in this stage of your life.

> *PRACTICE — transition: stretch, knees to chest, rocking on the spine*

Bring small movements into the fingers and toes, the wrists and ankles. Take a deep breath in as you extend the body into a long line, hands and feet pointing different directions. Exhaling, draw the arms down as you hug the knees into the chest, forehead coming up towards the knees. Inhaling, extend long. Exhaling, back into a ball — and repeat three more times with deep breathing.

If the environment is suitable, begin to rock backwards and forwards along the spine. Emptying the lungs completely on the forward, inhaling fully on the backwards. Up to fifteen rounds. If not suitable for you, you can slowly come into a sitting position as you wait for others to finish.

## Paschimottanasana — Contentment Under Challenge

> *PRACTICE — Paschimottanasana held for about five minutes*

Making these last two to three rounds, come up with the torso upright, the legs extended straight in front. Going to move into a forward bend and hold for some time: *paschimottanasana*.

If you have any back or nerve condition that is contraindicated for forward bending, then you can hold a more upright posture. Otherwise, take a deep breath in as you extend the hands towards the ceiling, and then, exhaling, slowly reach forward.

Find a position that you believe you can hold for five minutes. To hold with minimal or total absence of movement. An arrangement where you are not constantly pulling on your toes or feet or heels or ankles, but simply making contact.

Then bring the awareness completely into your body, and feel into what this pose, this *asana*, reveals. A new set of conditions or parameters. In what areas of the body is there approval? In what areas of the body may there be complaints? And to what capacity can you remain the witness to these inner conversations, and simply continue to hold?

There are at least two dimensions of *pratyahara*. One, where we move into a state of absence or silence from sensory input — we turn off the pain, or we deny the pleasure. And the second direction is to be totally within both, but not internally affected. Do you have a sense of which approach you tend to take in different situations — particularly this situation?

Shift the awareness to the natural breath. Does it indicate a preference, an opinion, about the current position? And if you were to hold this *asana* for two hours, in what way might you adjust the breath? Find a way of breathing that reflects a total acceptance of being held in this way.

And finally, be present with any mental dialogue or expression from within. Sometimes *asana*, even *pranayama*, are hard to sustain — not because of how they feel, but what they trigger or shift within us. Does holding this *asana* modify the mind in any particular way? And what is the emotional response to that?

Can you find an attitude that allows for total acceptance of holding for one more minute? An attitude leaning towards joy and contentment, rather than just endurance and austerity.

## Four Quotes

> *PRACTICE — seated meditation: listening to four quotes on contentment*

Mentally prepare to transition out and back into a comfortable meditative *asana*, making all the arrangements necessary to once again find physical and/or mental stillness for the next three minutes.

I read a few quotes, and upon hearing them, observe where and how they land within.

> By contentment you will have great gain. A contented mind is ever peaceful and joyous.
> — *[Swami Sivananda Saraswati, quoted in* Transforming Lobha*, p. 65]*

> *Santosha* is being happily content with whatever one has, and in whatever situation one is placed in. It is never a passive quality.
> — *[Swami Nirmalananda Saraswati,* Yogic Management of Cancer*, p. 196]*

> Contentment is a natural, spontaneous state of being, which arises when desires end.
> — *[Swami Satyananda Saraswati,* Rikhiapeeth Satsangs *Vol 5, p. 125]*

> Contentment does not deny effort. Contentment emphasises *purushartha*, the effort that you make to become better.
> — *[Swami Niranjanananda Saraswati,* The Yoga of Sage Vasishtha*, p. 34]*

Bring your awareness into the body — the body given to you in this lifetime.

## Closing

> *PRACTICE — closing: OM and Om Shanti ×3, palming*

Deepen the breath in preparation for chanting of the mantra *OM*, and *Om Shanti* three times. Concentrate the mind on the eyebrow centre, and from that gaze within, see an object of light, such as a steady golden flame. Slow, full breath in to chant the mantra *OM*.

> *CHANT — OM, then Om Shanti Shanti Shanti*

You can move into a round of palming. Taking a deep breath in, taking that warmth in, and then exhaling and lowering your hands when ready.

To watch y'all hold these *asanas* for long periods was very nice. Thank you for the effort you put in for that.

Okay. *Hari Om Tat Sat.* I hope y'all explore *santosha* in your own way. Stay warm until we meet again. *Jai Ho.*
