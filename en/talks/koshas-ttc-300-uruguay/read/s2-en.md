## Opening and Settling

*Are we waiting for anyone, Ale, or ready to begin? — No, you can start.*

Wonderful. Good morning to each and every one of you. It's good to see all your faces again. We continue in this exploration and connection to the *koshas*. This is the second session out of the four planned.

> *PRACTICE — Settling: rotation of awareness through the five koshas*

We take a minute or two just to bring ourselves physically, energetically, and mentally prepared for this session. Come into any position that allows you to be still for about two minutes. Truly comfortably still. It's recommended that the hands be in a *mudra* or a gesture, the mouth gently closed, and the eyes closed if safe to do so.

And without trying to feel or be any particular way, just notice how you are right now — and in which dimensions you are aware of yourself. It can sometimes be helpful to rotate your awareness through the five *koshas* in coming into contact with, or observing, yourself: the *Annamaya kosha*, or the physical body; the *Pranamaya kosha*, or the energetic body; the *Manomaya kosha*, or mental body; the *Vijnanamaya kosha*, or intuitive wisdom body; and the *Anandamaya kosha*, or blissful causal body.

We're going to rotate our awareness once more through these *koshas*. As I say the name of it, mentally repeat the Sanskrit name, and take a deep breath in and out of that layer. When you inhale, breathe fully into that space and allow it to expand outwards. When you exhale, breathe back out of that space and allow it to contract to a single point — for example, the eyebrow center or the heart center.

Deep breath in and out of *Annamaya kosha*. *Pranamaya kosha*. *Manomaya kosha*. *Vijnanamaya kosha*. *Anandamaya kosha*.

And one final breath, where you imagine breathing in from the grossest physical to the subtlest — *Anandamaya* — and back to the physical.

## Mantra: Saha Nāvavatu

Now we do a few practices. Pardon me — I'll repeat one mantra. You can remain still and listen to it; if you know it, you can repeat it with me mentally; if not, you receive it.

It's both a mantra and an intention. The intention is that in this session we don't distinguish between student and teacher — that we are learning from each other and from the *vidya*, or the knowledge itself — and that we do so with an attitude of openness, positivity, and non-hostility.

Place the awareness at the eyebrows and try to keep it there, at the center, or at the heart center. Visualize or feel there a lotus flower in bloom, and at the center of that lotus flower, a divine flame giving off light to the inner world.

> *Om Saha Nāvavatu, Saha Nau Bhunaktu, Saha Vīryam Karavāvahai, Tejasvi Nāvadhītamastu, Mā Vidviṣāvahai. Om Shanti Shanti Shanti. Hari Om.*

## Waking Practices — One per Kosha

> *PRACTICE — Annamaya kosha: palming and movement*

We bring our palms together in *prana mudra*, thumbs in contact with the heart. Take another deep breath in and out, feeling the torso expand and contract. Then move into a good round of palming, bringing heat between the palms. When you have warmth to share back to the eyes, bring them just in front of the eyelids — feeling the pressure of the palms against the forehead and cheekbones, sensing the warmth coming from the surface of the palms, noticing anything you see in this darkened space that we call *Chidakash*. Take a deep breath in through the eyes, into your head and brain; exhaling down the spine and the whole body.

We're going to do this two more times as a practice of *Annamaya kosha* — of just awakening our body, feeling our body, moving our body. For this next round, maintain for at least 15 to 20 seconds. Notice which muscles you feel, any fatigue you might feel. Change the position if you need. Keep rubbing for a few more seconds.

Covering the eyes once again, rotate your awareness through your senses: the inner visions or patterns that may appear in *Chidakash*; any sounds that you're aware of, external or internal; any tastes present in your mouth, the position of your tongue; the warmth coming from the palms anywhere on the face. And finally, through the nose, a deep breath in; then exhaling, release the hands.

And one final round, in a slightly more relaxed way. You can stretch the arms forward, or up at a diagonal — use movement. Find a position, find a rhythm that helps wake you up. And then start to speed up. And then speed up again. And a final round as fast as you can. Try to make a fire between your palms and bring it to your eyes — try and visualize that flame, that fire, maybe a favorite campfire. Try and feel that inner *prana* being lit, woken up. Exhale completely with the hands still on your eyes. Take a really deep breath in, guiding that breath down to the base of the torso, all the way up to the crown. And then exhaling, slide the hands down the face, down the body. Massage your arms, your legs, your stomach.

Be completely still for about a minute. Another 15 seconds — and notice where the predominant sensations can be felt, anywhere in or around the body. Is there any group of sensations you're aware of now that you weren't aware of five minutes ago? Following that logic, can you, through willpower, feel into more and more areas of your body at the same time?

> *PRACTICE — Pranamaya kosha: Kapalabhati*

We do a few practices now for the pranic system, also to help awaken. The first *pranayama* being *Kapalabhati*. Is there anyone who's never done the brain-cleansing breath? You can raise your hand. Everyone's done that? Awesome.

So we'll do maybe three rounds of 15, just one second apart. Unless you have a condition that contraindicates it, at the end of the first 15 breaths you're going to take a deep breath in and hold, with the chin just slightly down. And when you feel that the pressure has built sufficiently, then lift the chin and exhale in a controlled way. Awareness at the eyebrow center — imagine that each sharp breath out is like a hammer tapping at that point.

Begin the first round of 15. After 15, deep breath in and hold. Focus on the sensations, moment to moment. Exhaling when you feel it's time. Then total, natural, unregulated breath, awareness at the eyebrow center still. Begin your second round of 15. Holding the breath for a comfortable duration. Awareness in the area of *Chidakash*, experiencing moment-by-moment sensations. And the third and final round. Holding the breath. Really allow it to build up some pressure so that the sensations build up strongly as well. And remain totally connected to those sensations as you let the breath out; observe how they may change, where they lead you.

> *PRACTICE — Manomaya kosha: rotation through the senses*

Feel for any physical sensation throughout the whole body, moving the attention from one area to the next. Switch now to the awareness of sounds. Imagine you are like a radar, listening for sounds coming from any direction. As soon as you hear a sound, disconnect from it, find another. There is no good or bad sound. And finally, rest the awareness in the space of *Chidakash*, behind the forehead. Imagine that you are sitting inside the center of your head, looking at this mental screen, like it's a movie screen. What appears on that screen? Is it a void? Are there patterns, colors, or memories? Projections of the future?

> *PRACTICE — Vijnanamaya kosha: review since the last class*

And finally, go back to our last class. Remember anything that you can from that session — it doesn't have to be intellectual. Anything from that past session. And since that time, has there been awareness of the *koshas* in any way? Replay that movie of your life since that day until now. Which dimensions or layers were you predominantly moving and interacting and engaging with the world through?

We can end this short reflection by a final round of palming on your own. Open your eyes whenever you're ready.

## Connection — to the Self and to Others

Today we're going to go deeper into these *koshas* from the perspective of connecting to them — and also from the perspective of how we become sensitive to each *kosha*, with the intention of eventually becoming sensitive to others.

This is one of the expectations and hopes for those of us who have been teaching for some time: that yoga teachers be rooted in their personal connection, so that what is being shared is coming out of experience rather than just theory. It's very easy to get stuck in — not only stuck, but enamored, in love with — the theory and the words. But embodying that theory into actual experience requires exploration and sometimes work.

Okay, let me find the slides for today, and then we begin the new content. I use these slides loosely, just as a way for me to stay according to what I want to cover. But as I mentioned before, if an area catches your interest, I'm very happy to go into it in more detail.

So the theme of this session is connection: connection to the self, and eventually connection to others.

## Wakefulness Across the Koshas

Something I appreciate about your session being at 6:30 is that there's definitely a transition from sleeping to waking. And this idea of wakefulness is central to yoga, because the question can always arise: *how awake am I, truly?* And do you feel this at 6:30 in the morning? Minute by minute — maybe once the coffee begins to hit, maybe once the sun begins to rise — sometimes you begin to feel more awake, and sometimes you begin to feel like you're being pulled back into sleep.

So now let's break that up into the five layers. How awake does your body feel? How awake are the *pranas* — can you feel them actually moving through you, or present in any part of you? Do you find that you're breathing deeply, or shallow breathing? Rhythmically or arrhythmically? Could you jog around your neighborhood or take a lap on the bicycle, or would you prefer not to? And of course, on the mental level you're feeling right now — is this session something you can engage with, or is it a bit too soon in the day? At the level of the *Vijnanamaya kosha*, which is of patterns and accumulated wisdom: have programs for how you will move through the day already turned on, or not yet — so you're liable to make the same mistakes until you run into that situation that triggers you to wake up?

There are at least two types of alarms within us. One type are the ones that we set — they are sort of our awareness alarms. The second are the ones that nature has set for us, out of love for us. It's my understanding that we want to gradually replace all of nature's alarms with our own alarms, with our own programming.

Going back to this idea of wakefulness: in each of these five *koshas*, what is the limit of how much they can be awakened? And can we imagine the experience of being human when we start to move toward their full potential — not only individually, but simultaneously? That is the invitation I have for you, and in fact I think it's the invitation that the knowledge of yoga has for all of us.

If you've ever experienced being acutely awake in any one of these dimensions — how do you sustain it? How do you bring the other *koshas* into that same state of wakefulness? By wakefulness, I don't mean moving or active, but rather present and aware. This means that when you're sleeping, you know that you're sleeping. That's an experiment that you must take on and try to experience: when you're sleeping, wake up in that state of sleep and know, *I am sleeping.* Techniques like *Yoga Nidra* and lucid dreaming help move us into such an experience.

## Property of the Koshas: Scale

Let's look at some properties of the *koshas*, just to move ourselves into their spaces. One is scale.

What are the dimensions of the physical body? For most of us, it's probably one and a half to two meters maximum in any dimension; on the third dimension, it's usually only a few inches, less than a foot. Individuals who are really aware of their scale are really fun to watch — like kids at a playground, or dancers, rock climbers, swimmers, people who like to go through caves — because a full awareness of our three-dimensional self really allows us to play in three-dimensional reality.

If we go to the *Pranamaya kosha*, what is its scale? Recently I was in Uruguay, in the hills above Rocha — beautiful hill country, some of the blackest sky I've ever seen because of minimal light pollution. And in that blackness, a host, a friend, brought out some very high-quality infrared glasses. From that blackness emerged these bright red blobs in the distance — undifferentiated shapes, circular outlines. And you can imagine what they were: they were cows, they were foxes, they were coyotes. That means the heat coming from those beings' bodies was traveling hundreds of meters to these infrared glasses. The scale of the *prana* being emitted from those animals covered entire hillsides. That's significant, because sometimes we forget that we have a dimension of ourselves that is actually, physically, scientifically reaching hundreds of meters into the distance.

And now let's move to the *Manomaya kosha*. What is its scale? To start with, it taps into the fourth dimension of time. My mind can travel to the past and it can travel to the future. It can travel along the fifth dimension to things that have never happened but are only imagined. And it can travel in the opposite direction — to things that have never happened but will absolutely happen if I just follow the steps. Even though I'm in Romania now, my mind can be completely present in my house in Nepal and actually feel the different objects of the rooms, because I know that space well. The scale of the human mind is something that seems to have given us a unique place on Earth — for better and for worse.

What, now, is the scale of the *Vijnanamaya kosha*, seeing that so far each new *kosha* is orders of magnitude larger than the prior? The *Vijnanamaya kosha* is the sphere of not only all knowledge that we have accumulated, but anything that can be known or conceived. It represents experience that somehow exists even if not within our experience yet. That is a really large space. This is the space where individuals like Einstein say they've had these jumps, these leaps in intuition. It's the space where — it's said — when we think of someone and then they write to us or send us a message the next day, it comes from. The space where prophets, or intuitives, seem to be able to accurately predict what's going to happen.

Consider, in your life, the way that we normally learn something. We have an experience; it's either pleasurable or unpleasurable. And through a repeated series of this, some pattern gets locked in, and we have a teaching that we now hold. Each repetition is a step until we integrate this in a total way. So between inexperienced and experienced, I might need hundreds of steps before I really learn something. Imagine that all those steps live in *Manomaya kosha*. Now imagine there was some way that you could avoid all those steps and somehow just fly to that final lesson in *Vijnanamaya kosha*. We are humans — we like to avoid sitting in a wagon for three years, or sitting in a boat for six months, and to directly fly to our destination. Consider the time that's saved, the energy that's saved, the risks that are saved.

How do we do that at the *kosha* level? How do we evolve in a way to start to access *Vijnanamaya kosha* more directly, avoiding the pitfalls of *Manomaya kosha*? This is where I find the relevance of thinking about these *koshas* outside of the theoretical plane.

Finally, the scale of the *Anandamaya kosha* — I'm not sure how to talk about. Because it is the causal dimension, it has essentially an infinite scale, but it doesn't even make sense to give it a label. It's that potential from where all these other four *koshas* can emerge.

## Property of the Koshas: Density

One more physical quality to look at is density. The physical body certainly has density — you can't easily put your fingers through your body, unless you're a Kung Fu master (and then you're probably causing great damage to someone).

The *Pranamaya kosha*, being energetic in nature, is far less dense. In fact, it's very helpful to think of it as fluid. You've probably worked with the *nadis* — the pathways that *prana* flows through in the body. That's what it means: a flow, a route through which something can flow. In Nepali, a river is a *nadi*. So a *nadi* is a river of *prana*. And if you've never visualized or tried to feel your *Pranamaya kosha*, that is a very simple way to connect with it: do a dynamic *asana* sequence, like *Surya Namaskar*, for multiple rounds, and then be completely still and feel for the different *nadis* flowing through the body. There are major ones and minor ones. When we become familiar with the minor ones, you can start to exercise control over their flow.

Did you ever do that as kids — when it started to rain, build dams? Dams are when you block water and force the water to move another way. Any dam builders in here? A few, okay — it was usually a very boy thing, but there's no reason it has to be.

One time, in the woods behind my house, I found a pond with a few fish. The water was very brown from the tannins in the leaves. And I saw that a number of these leaves had been blocking some of the water flowing into the main pool. So I cleared that block, and more water started to flow in, and the sound increased, and it was lovely. I walked a few more meters up the hill and found another block of fallen leaves, and I removed that, and the water began to flow even more. I spent the next two hours clearing all the leaf-blocks from this small stream until I reached the top of the hill. And the next day, when I came back to that same place, the water was flowing clearly. And that pond was incredible — it looked like there were a thousand times more fish.

That was the teaching from *Prakriti*, or nature, to me about what we're doing when we work with *Pranamaya kosha*. We clear the blocks, we increase the flow. When we do that, there will be a huge increase in life. And we're just mediums of life, just conduits for life force. I know it's easy to think of ourselves as a personality — but another perspective is that we're just speakers for this music called life. And when our speakers are at max volume, what does that feel like, not only for ourselves, but for our loved ones, for our community?

I'll leave it as an exercise for yourself: what does the density of the remaining *koshas* look and feel like? Is there anywhere that the mind cannot go? That's a real question — and you can't answer it until you sincerely try to travel with the mind into different spaces and dimensions. Is there anything that the space of knowledge does not touch or access? What are its limits and boundaries? And the same question for *Anandamaya kosha*.

Let's take a pause there. Take a couple of deep breaths. If you've been sitting for a while, maybe you'd like to stand up and stretch the whole body in a long line, get some movement in. We'll pick up in a minute.

*(Brief check-in.)* How's it going, Ale? Is this too basic? — No, I think it's amazing. — I want to include ideas that aren't covered usually with the *koshas*, because I find they're kept too dry and inaccessible. So the motivation is, I'm really inviting each of you to think about each *kosha*, explore each *kosha*. They're really mind-blowing as you begin to shift in and between them. *Koshas* are not ideas. They are real spaces, real fields.

## Nourishment of Each Kosha

A really interesting aspect, especially in the last few years for me, is: if these *koshas* are here — which they are — what are they nourished by? What influences their health? One of the reasons we can even ask that question is because all of us experience different states of physical health, and variations in mental health and well-being. We definitely see these variations in our family, in the community, even in the world. That's at least two out of the five dimensions. So does that apply to the energetic body as well — does it have variations, and can it be nourished or undernourished? What does it even mean for the intuitive dimension to be fed or malnourished? And similarly for the causal dimension?

Let's start with the physical body. When do we actually feel nourished and whole and happy physically? This is a question for yourself — I really want you to answer it. What do I need to get into that bubbly, fuzzy, cozy, yummy, happy feeling? If you can't think about that and start to feel that feeling, I claim you're not connecting with that in real life.

If I think about an oven-baked frozen pizza next to the fireplace, with my arm around someone I love — a brother or a friend — cracking jokes, feeling that in my belly, I start to feel happy right now just talking about it. In *Ayurveda*, I'm *vata*. I love touch. I'm nourished by tactile sensations. I'm a favorite uncle because I wrestle with the kids; I'm fun in a martial arts school; I'm in this class because I'm jumping everywhere trying to touch you. For others, it will be beautiful art, or the right tastes throughout the day. So we have to know what nourishes our whole physical body — not just nutrition and vitamins, but the multiple dimensions that our body experiences, the senses.

Now for the pranic dimension. What nourishes it? Some obvious answers that are probably within our experience: breath, rest, nature. Another way to think about this is: where do you observe high-quality *prana* in your spaces? What do I mean by high-quality *prana*? Some indicators are vibrancy. Another hint is harmony. Another indication may be sustainability, or endurance. Why does sitting under a tree that's a hundred years old create a feeling at the pranic level? To be in the presence of a being that has learned how to survive in a calm way — that has learned to channel energy through its whole being and share it with everyone without discrimination. These are some of the ways we want to learn to read *prana* in our environment — including from people.

When you sit down in front of a room of students and you're doing *Shantipat*, what are you looking for? When you ask individuals to become still, usually three layers immediately emerge: the layer that refuses, or is unable, to be still; the layer that moves into unconsciousness the moment of becoming still; and the layer that stays attentively present.

## The Gunas

These three qualities are everywhere in nature, in every single atom. We refer to them as the *gunas*: *tamas*, *rajas*, and *sattva* — inertia, activity, and harmony. When we wake up first thing in the morning, we have a lot of *tamas*, a lot of inertia. None of these are bad or good — they're just qualities. When they come out of balance with the environment and the situation we're in, they tend to cause karmic problems.

If you are just waking up and predominantly *tamasic*, and your puppy runs onto the bed and bounces on your face, there is a *tamas–rajas* incompatibility there. But because of that puppy's *rajasic* quality, it will probably influence your *tamas* and start to wake you up. And you don't want that puppy bouncing around the living room all day, so you take it on a walk so it has a way to channel its energy. When you both return home, hopefully everyone is more *sattvic* — everyone is either more awake or more calm.

When we work with the *koshas*, always read the *gunas*. Which *guna* is predominant in this *kosha* right now? Because as soon as you have a read, you can bring it back into balance.

Every single yogic practice is designed to either stimulate, tranquilize, or maintain balance of each of the *koshas*. This completely changes the way that we relate to yoga and the various practices of the yogic branches. They're no longer something to be done, but algorithms to tune ourselves to any moment in time and situation. And not only can the yoga practices tune or influence the *gunas* in each *kosha* — they can expand or contract our awareness within each one of them. When you put all these together, we suddenly now have the capacity to really live and move through our multiple dimensions in a really holistic way.

So: *Manomaya kosha* — what is *tamasic* for it? What is *rajasic* for it? What is *sattvic* for it? What lifestyle choices pull our minds way out and make us hyper-expansive? And which lifestyle choices make us ultra-focused — for better or for worse? What have we recognized that nourishes and develops, or evolves, our intuitive dimension?

## The Intuitive Body — The Buddhist Monk

I lived with a Buddhist monk who was very intuitive. I asked him about it, and he said: *I wake up and I make a prediction about everything.* And what I observed is — some things I'm very good about predicting, and other things I'm not; some things I get wrong all the time. He was guessing what meals he would eat that day, who he would meet, what the weather would be — everything. And the more he exercised this capacity, the more accurate he got, and the more aware he became about everything he was making predictions about.

To the point where he could read people's inner emotions and energies like I've never seen before. He could get into someone's mind, usually within three minutes, and have them read — and have them crying, just because he would touch an issue that they were completely in denial or unaware of.

Once a skeptic came and said, *I have an injury somewhere in my body; I want you to find it.* And in front of me, he just put a hand on their palm and sat still for a few minutes. And then he said, *I think you have had a big injury around your elbows.* And the person was really shocked, because they hadn't revealed anything. When I asked how he did it, he said: *I've spent years, decades, bringing awareness to the tips of my fingers and through my whole body. And when I touch someone else, all I do is extend my awareness into their body — like their body is my body. I just push it a little bit farther.*

There's nothing, according to the laws of physics, that says that isn't possible — that's exactly how most of our instruments work for detecting things. We just have to exercise the capacity. Because the physical dimension is so easy to exercise, physical yoga has taken over 99% of the world. But we have four other *koshas* to be exercised, developed to the same capacity. So, as practitioners and teachers, please don't get stuck in the physical, or even the pranic dimension.

## Imbalance, Regulation, and the Elements

So this slide is what we were talking about: the overfed, the underfed, or the fed-with-incorrect-inputs — meaning inputs that take us out of balance, versus inputs that bring us into balance. And by balance or imbalance, I also mean integrated and intrinsically strong, versus — what's the word opposite of integrated? The popular word these days is *dysregulated*. There's nothing wrong with being dysregulated, except it creates so many gaps within us that it's easy to become unwound. And if we're in dysregulated states for a long time, sometimes it can even break us down — hence these terms like a "nervous breakdown" — often because we're fighting that dysregulation and the tension is unsustainable.

So we have two options, two good options. One is: let's dysregulate completely and have a full breakdown, and then build back up. That is a very challenging path, but very healing if you survive it. Or, we can skillfully remain regulated and hold the harmonious tension that is life.

I won't go into this next slide so much, but I just want to drop the seed on you — I'll share all these resources with Ale. It's to consider the elements, the five elements, as they relate to the *koshas*. This is a model. This is not a claim of reality, so don't take it literally; look for such patterns in life, in everyday interactions. When we're feeling anxiety, which *kosha* does anxiety intrinsically belong to? It's the mind — it's airy. So what is the antidote? You look to something like the earth, the *Annamaya kosha*: cold showers, gardening, heavy blankets. So there are lots of solutions embedded in looking at the *koshas* overlaid with other aspects of yoga metaphysics.

## Bringing It Into a Class

If you're teaching a yoga class full of people who just got out of the office on a Friday afternoon, maybe the first practice you don't want to do is a lot of dynamic movement. It might be to slow everything down and anchor, and then bring back regulated movement — or regulated breathing, or systematic thinking, sustained concentration — or pattern recognition and review of the week: what went well, what didn't go so well. Or not thinking about anything, or doing anything, but simply connecting to the fact that we're still here — the experience of *I am*, which is from the *Anandamaya kosha*. That is a very powerful medicine.

These next slides — which we won't get into in depth now, because we're out of time — start to enter the idea of: knowing more about ourselves, how can we connect with someone outside of ourselves?

There's that famous quote from Gandhi: *be the change you wish to see in the world.* It's a very nice quote that aligns with yoga philosophy, which is: don't teach anything that you haven't touched yourself. I admit to a level of hypocrisy in that — but the continuous goal is to be more established in the practices, in direct experience. And that same invitation extends to all of you: use these moments and time-frames of learning to really dive deep into the practices, and to experiment, to keep a journal, and to find your boundaries of experience.
