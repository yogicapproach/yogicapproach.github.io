## Elements, indriyas, and mantra as a tool of Tantra
[13:30]

One of the ideas we'd left off with last time is sound and mantras and their influence on the koshas.

According to yoga philosophy and metaphysics, the elements have different densities, and each has a corresponding sensory pathway or sense organ, which we call *indriyas*. **Earth** is considered the heaviest, and associates with smell. **Water** is less dense and corresponds to taste. **Fire** is lighter than water and corresponds to sight and light. Then comes **air** or wind, which corresponds to touch. Then we have **space** or ether, which corresponds to sound. And finally the subtlest is the **mind**. Because sound is one of the subtlest sensory pathways, it's considered to pervade the others.

We leverage that through practices that are very old — they predate yoga; they come from a time of tantric discoveries. So mantra is a tool of *Tantra*, which has been adopted by yoga, which has been adopted by various religions. By *Tantra* I mean anything that expands consciousness and liberates energy — that's the definition, loosely.

One of the practices we'll do today is a short **Yoga Nidra**, which also utilizes sound: it allows the practitioner to be completely still and, in advanced stages, guided by external or internal sound.

## The three morning mantras: subconscious, affirmation, sankalpa
[17:08]

Last time you heard three mantras recited just a few times each. In the tradition I've done most of my training with — the **Satyananda Yoga** tradition — these three mantras are done instantly upon waking. The reason is that at the time of waking, we still have contact with our subconscious. I'm sure you've experienced this: when you wake up, you still have access to dreams. But usually, as soon as we start to move — or to talk, think, or interact with the outside world — those dreams slip away faster. It's incredible to me that you could have been a superhero, or in a nightmare, seconds before, and after waking and moving, that entire world can be totally inaccessible — to the point where you don't remember a single detail.

And yet that layer of the mind — the subconscious — ancient and modern psychology says could be influencing **70% or more of our waking life**. We believe, and may have experienced, that subconscious impressions influence our waking life — which is to say they influence all four koshas, and maybe all five.

So how can we start to create a positive feedback loop that implants positive impressions into the subconscious mind while it's still accessible? One of the first ways is to put in **positive affirmations** right upon waking, and then to reinforce that positive seed throughout the day or week through yogic practices and lifestyle. We'll be doing that with the full version of these three morning mantras.

In a group this large, I understand these mantras may be new — even the idea may not resonate. *[Some of you practiced them with Jorge on Saturday, so you know them.]*

Today I want to emphasize the **setup** of the mantras: the *sankalpa* or affirmation, and in particular the **waking-up of the emotional feeling** of that *sankalpa*, and the **visualization** of it. Both of these skills are prerequisites in the higher branches of yoga, such as *Bhakti Yoga* and certainly *Kriya Yoga*. So as we chant — whether out loud or just listening — keep bringing the mind back to the affirmation for the mantra.

For example, **Mahāmṛtyuñjaya** is an ancient mantra for total well-being and protection. What does that look like to you? What does it actually feel like? When is the last time you felt great in all layers of your being? Was it when you were five years old and could jump around like a spring from couch to couch? Was it when you were a young adult, fully in your body, mind sharp, confident, safe in the world? Simultaneously, if there's a present-day condition that keeps you from feeling whole, can you imagine that part of you that could use healing — in its healed state, its state of well-being?

In those first moments before the mantra, I really want you to put that energy in — the capacity of the mind to imagine and to *feel* what it's imagining — because it can take years, decades, to awaken that capacity to its full potential. From the yogic perspective, we'd like to feel cold when we think cold, start sweating when we think hot, feel heavy when we think heavy, feel light when we think light — and this goes for any example we can imagine. We already do this: if we see something sensual, usually there are signs in the body that we're starting to feel it. But we want to stretch and apply this ability — to feel according to what we're thinking — to anything we choose, at will, directing it toward our positive growth.

The last thing I'll say: look into the book **_The Brain That Changes Itself_** by Norman Doidge. It's amazing — modern-day case studies that validate ancient claims, such as that visualization can actually change the body. *(Ale will write and share the title.)*

## 32 Names of Durgā ×3 (heart center / causal body)
[39:52]

For the final mantra, we bring our awareness down to the **heart center** at the sternum, and connect with the **causal body**.

Pauli, in the last class, had asked what the practices are for the *Vijñānamaya kosha* and the *Anandamaya kosha*. Perfecting the art of making *sankalpas* is itself a practice of the causal body, of *Anandamaya kosha* — because it requires this patient revision of *what is it that I really, really want to experience, at its seed?* Underneath the words, underneath the conditioning — what is the deepest part of myself really asking to experience?

I equate the mantras of **Durgā** to the causal body, because she represents the causal force from which all of this manifest reality — including consciousness — comes. ## Yoga Nidra — body & sound awareness, sankalpa
[61:01]

When finished, come to lie in **Shavasana**, ideally with the head facing the laptop and feet away, so the ears are closest to the speakers. Ensure you'll be able to remain warm for the next 10 to 15 minutes. Legs hip-width apart, wide enough that the sacrum and the backs of the buttocks can really settle into the floor, legs rolling outward as comfortable for the hips. Palms turned upward and far enough from the body that the shoulder blades and shoulders can settle. *(If you're sitting, you can still practice with the eyes closed, moving the awareness as directed.)*

As soon as you're able, find a stillness within the position. Feel into the whole physical body as it is right now — the legs, arms, torso, neck, and head; the bones, muscles, fluids, organs. Find and feel the exact points of contact between body and floor — along the backs of the legs, the backs of the arms, the back of the torso, the neck and head, between the whole body and the floor.

Mentally tell yourself to let go of your body — to release its whole weight onto the earth below; to release any management of the breath so it too becomes natural and spontaneous. And as the body, breath, and mind settle on their own, take the awareness into the space around you.

Imagining yourself to be a sensitive radar, note any sound you hear; hearing it, disconnect and find another. Hearing another, let it go, find another — keeping the mind active, moving from sound to sound. Listen for the faintest sound in the distance; hearing it, find another faint sound. Then bring that awareness of hearing into your own body and listen for internal sounds — the heart, the breath, the nervous system. Listen and feel for the silence between these rhythmic patterns: between two heartbeats there is stillness and silence; between two breaths there is stillness and silence. Wait for that moment and observe it.

This is the time to repeat your **Sankalpa** — a positive idea that represents a deep transformation you wish to bring to yourself. *[~68:59]* If you're not sure what it is, you can make the sankalpa: *"I intuit my true sankalpa; I become aware of my deepest life calling, my soul's desire."*

The awareness will now be taken on a journey through the body — hearing the name of a body part, touch the mind to that area. There's no need to move any body part, only awareness to that point. *[Rotation of consciousness: right hand, wrist, forearm, elbow, upper arm, shoulder, armpit, right side of torso, right hip, thigh, knee, shin, right foot; the same on the left side; backs of the legs, buttocks, back of the torso, backs of the arms, back of the neck and head; the whole face, the front torso, the pelvic region, arms and legs; the whole right side, the whole left side, the whole body.]* Experience the whole body from tiptoe to crown of head.

Within the stillness of the physical body, the natural breath has been flowing on its own. Find, feel, and observe its movements. With the awareness at the entrance of the nasal passages, feel each breath entering and exiting the body. Count each natural breath backwards from five to one — for example, "five, awareness of breathing in; five, awareness of breathing out; four, breathing in…" — until you reach zero. Wherever you are in that count, let it go.

## Antarmouna — witnessing the mind, then externalizing
[74:51]

Be aware of any activity or non-activity in the mind. Look into that mental screen of *chidakasha* behind the forehead and closed eyes; whatever may appear there, be the **observer, the witness**.

Imagine the lightness of the air that surrounds the physical body. Feel the heaviness of the body lying on the floor.

Begin to inhale more intentionally, deeper in both directions — externalizing to reconnect with the sounds around you, the smells, the temperature in the room, with each deepening breath. Small movements in the fingers and toes, the wrists and ankles. Give yourself a long stretch, as if waking up in bed in the morning. Hug the knees in toward the chest, rocking side to side, and eventually come up into a comfortable sitting position. Keep the eyes closed and the body still for a few moments, just noticing any effects of this short relaxation. Rotate your awareness through the five koshas. Then do a round of palming to complete the externalization, lowering the hands when you're ready.

## Closing metaphor — the table of the five koshas
[78:10]

I'll close with a final example that's worked really well for me in the past few years. *(This AI image made a very weird table, and since I tend to make my slides last-minute, I haven't updated it — but imagine there are only four legs, like a normal table.)*

I had an experience in the last few years where I was physically and energetically at my peak — even mentally — but emotionally, I'd been ignoring things for a number of years. You can imagine a table, even with three legs, will balance — until you knock out another leg, or put something on the corner that doesn't have a leg under it. And so it happened in my life, which was a blessing: a few things happened at the same time that both shook the other legs *and* put a lot of emotional weight on the one corner that wasn't supported. And the table — which represents myself — came crashing down.

It was a blessing because it forced me to re-evaluate all aspects of my life, and which parts of myself I'd been deeply ignoring such that it would create such a collapse. During that time I started connecting with private students who were going through their own life collapse, so it was an interesting opportunity to test many aspects of yoga in a real way. Now it's part of my daily, weekly, and monthly self-evaluation: what is the condition of each of these legs?

The first four koshas — *Annamaya, Pranamaya, Manomaya, and Vijñānamaya* — represent the four legs, and *Anandamaya* is the balance, the stability of the whole table. I'm still finding this metaphor works for me. For example, I see how, due to my own ambitiousness and conditioning, I try to make these legs very, very long: I want to be super strong, fit, and attractive; to work all day and sleep as little as possible; when I do sleep, to have lucid dreams and visit gurus of my past lives; to forget nothing; to manage multiple projects and read some number of books a year while learning multiple languages; to be intuitive enough to predict the future and empathetic enough to know how all my loved ones feel and what they need.

This vision of myself is great — until one thing needs to be taken from the top of the table, or the table shakes and anything on top falls; because when they do fall, they fall a really long way. The opposite could be to cultivate a lot of simplicity and humility, building a stable table with very short legs — but that also risks other people using my table as a stepping stool.

I use an internal and external journal to see where the legs of this table are, and I recommend you find a system that works for you to really connect with the state of each of your koshas — and a model that really works for you, whether it's a car, a table, a bridge, whatever.
