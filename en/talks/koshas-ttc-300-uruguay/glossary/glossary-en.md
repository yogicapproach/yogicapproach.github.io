# Glossary of Terms
## From the Koshas Teacher-Training with Satchidananda

## A

**Akasha / Akashic records** — *Sanskrit.* Space or ether; the subtlest element, paired with sound. The Akashic records are offered as an image of the *Vijnanamaya Kosha* — "a library where anything that can be known can be accessed," where adepts "know which floor, which shelf, which book." [3] See also: *Vijnanamaya Kosha*.

**Anandamaya Kosha** (आनन्दमय) — *Sanskrit.* The fifth and subtlest sheath; the bliss or causal body. *Ananda* is "bliss or beatitude, but also the uniform, the pure, that which does not change." [1] Not a layer beside the others but the ground from which they emerge — "that potential from where all these other four koshas can emerge," of essentially infinite scale. [2] Experienced as "baseline coherence — a state of non-duality," and as the simple experience of "I am," which is "a very powerful medicine." [1], [2] In the closing metaphor it is "the balance, the stability of the whole table." [4] See also: *Kosha*, *Sankalpa*.

**Annamaya Kosha** (अन्नमय) — *Sanskrit.* The first and densest sheath; the physical or "food" body (*anna* = food). "The physical body is condensed energy." [1] Likened to a whirlpool — "not different from the water… but it has a definite form and structure" — and to earth, "the antidote" to airy anxiety: "cold showers, gardening, heavy blankets." [1], [2] The grossest and easiest layer to work with; strengthen it "and the others begin to open up — on their own, when we're ready." [3], [4] See also: *Kosha*, *Maya*.

**Antarmouna** — *Sanskrit.* "Inner silence"; a meditation of rotating awareness through the senses — physical sensation, then sound "like a radar," then resting on the mental screen of *Chidakasha* and witnessing whatever appears. Used to wake and observe the *Manomaya Kosha*. [2], [4] See also: *Chidakasha*, *Manomaya Kosha*.

**Asana** — *Sanskrit.* A yoga posture. In this series asanas are "never just what appears on the surface as physical exercise" — they are "designed in larger patterns to touch multiple layers at the same time": movement reaches Annamaya, breath and exertion Pranamaya, coordination and counting Manomaya. [1], [4] See also: *Laghoo Shankhaprakshalana*, *Surya Namaskar*.

## B

**Bhakti Yoga** — *Sanskrit.* The yoga of devotion and emotion; its tools include kirtan, *japa*, and *Pratipaksha Bhavana*. The trained capacity to "feel what you imagine" is named a prerequisite for Bhakti and *Kriya Yoga*. [1], [3], [4] See also: *Pratipaksha Bhavana*.

## C

**Chakra** — *Sanskrit.* An energy center along the body's axis. In the sound teaching, "each chakra's petals are the sounds of prana entering that junction of the body." [3] *Manipura* (navel) anchors the Mahamrityunjaya mantra; the eyebrow / guru chakra is associated with intuition. See also: *Manipura*, *Nadi*.

**Chidakasha** — *Sanskrit.* The "mind-space" or dark inner screen behind the forehead. "Rest the awareness in the space of Chidakash… looking at this mental screen like a movie screen. What appears? A void? Patterns, colors, memories?" [2] The resting-place for *Antarmouna* and for touching the bliss body. [2], [3], [4] See also: *Antarmouna*.

## D

**Drashta** — *Sanskrit.* The observer or witness. "One of the pillars of practicing or teaching yoga is the quality of being the observer — the drashta": knowing the purpose of practices and "giving space for someone to have an experience, rather than overly directing it." [3] It must start from one's own grounded self-awareness, so as to read the landscape in front of you "with minimal projection, minimal guessing."

**Durga** — *Sanskrit.* The Mother in her fierce, protective form; here "the causal force from which all of this manifest reality — including consciousness — comes." [4] Her mantra, the 32 Names of Durga (*Durga Dvatrimsat Namamala*), is offered to the Anandamaya (causal) body at the heart center. [3], [4] See also: *Anandamaya Kosha*, *Mantra*.

## G

**Gayatri Mantra** — *Sanskrit.* A mantra offered to the mental and wisdom koshas — held at the heart (Session 3) or the eyebrow center (Session 4) with a sankalpa "to connect with inner light, intuition and wisdom," breathing in "as if the light of the morning sun fills your whole self with radiant luminosity." [3], [4] See also: *Mantra*, *Manomaya Kosha*, *Vijnanamaya Kosha*.

**Gunas** — *Sanskrit.* The three qualities of nature: *tamas* (inertia, heaviness), *rajas* (activity, passion), *sattva* (harmony, clarity). "None is good or bad; they only cause problems when out of balance with the situation." The instruction is to "always read the gunas — which guna is predominant in this kosha right now?" — so that every practice becomes "an algorithm to tune ourselves to any moment." [2] See also: *Sadhana*.

## H

**Hari Om Tat Sat** — *Sanskrit.* The salutation and benediction that closes each settling and practice throughout the series, sounded after the palming and OM. [1], [2], [3], [4] See also: *OM*.

**Hatha Yoga** — *Sanskrit.* The yoga of body and breath — asana, pranayama and the *shatkarmas* — through which "we touch the autonomic systems and develop control over them." The first layer of work, bringing awareness to the physical and pranic bodies. [3] See also: *Shatkarma*, *Pranayama*.

## K

**Kapalabhati** — *Sanskrit.* A pranayama of rapid, forceful exhalations, "each exhalation like a hammer tapping" at the eyebrow center — done in "three rounds of fifteen with retention," or with whole-body shaking "like a puppet" to stimulate the pranas before dropping into stillness. [2], [3] See also: *Pranayama*.

**Karma Yoga** — *Sanskrit.* The yoga of selfless action and service (*seva*); its practice includes "watching the ego in interactions." [3]

**Kosha** — *Sanskrit.* A "sheath" or layer of the human being — "a layer, something that covers." The five koshas run from gross to subtle — physical, energetic, mental, wisdom, bliss — and are best held "not as five boxes to memorise" but "as a set of lenses… learn to change the lens on demand." [1] They are compared by scale and density, each "orders of magnitude larger than the prior." [2], [3], [4] See also: *Maya*.

**Kriya Yoga** — *Sanskrit.* A higher branch that awakens "deep psychic energies" and "influences all koshas"; the capacity to feel and visualize what the mind imagines is named among its prerequisites. [3], [4]

## L

**Laghoo Shankhaprakshalana** — *Sanskrit.* The "short" digestive cleanse: a complete set of five asanas — spinal stretch, side-bend, waist-twist, cobra-twist and squat-twist — that with salt water flushes the digestive tract ("the body as a laundry machine"), and done dry brings awareness straight to the Annamaya kosha. [4] See also: *Asana*, *Shatkarma*.

## M

**Mahamrityunjaya Mantra** — *Sanskrit.* A mantra offered to the grosser koshas, with awareness at the navel center (*Manipura*) and a sankalpa of "a field of safety and protection… vitality and health." One breathes in through the navel and out in reverse as it is chanted. [3], [4] See also: *Mantra*, *Manipura*.

**Manipura** — *Sanskrit.* The navel chakra, "the trigger point… which regulates prana through the whole body and brings vitality and health"; the anchor point for the Mahamrityunjaya mantra. [3], [4] See also: *Chakra*.

**Manomaya Kosha** (मनोमय) — *Sanskrit.* The third, mental sheath (*manas* = mind), split into a lower mind (perception, memory, cognition) and a higher mind (intellect, discernment / *viveka*). Its modern lens is "attention management"; its scale reaches "the fourth dimension of time" and "a fifth dimension of the imagined." [1], [2] Fertile "soil," in ancient and modern psychology alike, for planting *sankalpa*. [3], [4] See also: *Kosha*, *Sankalpa*.

**Mantra** — *Sanskrit.* A sacred sound held with a *sankalpa*; "a tool of Tantra," reaching every kosha because "sound is completely connected to the pranic level, and prana is life." Chanting a mantra is "playing the pranic keyboard of your subtle and gross nervous system," since "each Sanskrit letter maps to a leaf of the nerve structures." [3], [4] Intuited by *rishis*, mantras "largely bypass the intellect." See also: *OM*, *Rishi*, *Tantra*.

**Maya** — *Sanskrit.* In the kosha compounds, "made of" or "pervaded by" — also "a layer" or "veil." Each kosha is a *maya*, a sheath woven of a particular substance around the self. [1] See also: *Kosha*.

**Merudandasana** — *Sanskrit.* A balancing asana used to contain and channel a rising emotion — giving the energy "the largest, safest container to express itself and move out" rather than shutting the feeling down. [3] See also: *Manomaya Kosha*.

## N

**Nadi** — *Sanskrit.* A channel through which prana flows. "In Nepali, a river is a nadi. So a nadi is a river of prana." Clearing the "blocks" in these rivers "increases the flow… and life increases." [2], [3] See also: *Prana*, *Nadi Shodhana*.

**Nadi Shodhana** — *Sanskrit.* Alternate-nostril breathing (*Anuloma Viloma*) that balances the nadis — "one pranayama you can do your whole life," "empirically observed to harmonize the sympathetic and parasympathetic nervous systems." [3] See also: *Nadi*, *Pranayama*.

## O

**OM (Aum)** — *Sanskrit.* The primal mantra, "considered the first manifest aspect of unmanifest reality" (echoed by "In the beginning was the Word"). Chanted three times, "feeling the vibration move through mouth, throat and lips and out into the universe," at the opening and close of every session. [1], [2], [3], [4] See also: *Mantra*, *Hari Om Tat Sat*.

## P

**Prana** — *Sanskrit.* Vital force, life-energy; "prana is life," and "all life responds to sound and frequency" because sound is connected to prana. Seen through infrared as body-heat "reaching hundreds of meters into the distance," it flows through the *nadis*. [1], [2], [3] See also: *Pranamaya Kosha*, *Nadi*.

**Pranam Mudra** — *Sanskrit.* The joined-palms gesture of salutation (*pranam* ≈ "greetings" in Hindi and Nepali), used to open and close practices — "not to be confused with Pranamaya Mudra." Also heard as Namaskar Mudra, "the mudra we use when we say namaskar or namaste." [4]

**Pranamaya Kosha** (प्राणमय) — *Sanskrit.* The second, energetic sheath (*prana* = vital force). Subtler and more fluid than the body; because "the pranic level pervades every layer," it is the channel through which a mantra reaches all the others. Its modern lenses: "state transition" (Session 1) and a scale of "hundreds of meters" (Session 2). [1], [2], [3], [4] See also: *Kosha*, *Prana*, *Nadi*.

**Pranayama** — *Sanskrit.* Breathing practices that work the energetic body — "there are over thirty: some to stimulate prana, some to tranquilize, some to harmonize." Named in the series: *Kapalabhati*, *Nadi Shodhana* (Anuloma Viloma), Ujjayi and Bhramari. [1], [3] See also: *Kapalabhati*, *Nadi Shodhana*.

**Pratipaksha Bhavana** — *Sanskrit.* A Bhakti / Raja practice of "willfully generating opposite emotions" — cultivating a positive feeling to counter a negative one. [3] See also: *Bhakti Yoga*.

## R

**Raja Yoga** — *Sanskrit.* The yoga of the mind, working through *pratyahara* (sense-withdrawal) and *dharana* (concentration) to train and hold attention. [1], [3] See also: *Manomaya Kosha*.

**Rishi** — *Sanskrit.* A seer; the "spiritual scientists of the past" who intuited the mantras and verses. "These mantras were intuited by rishis… they resonated with others and survived until now because of their benefit." [3] See also: *Mantra*.

## S

**Sadhana** — *Sanskrit.* Personal spiritual practice. The series' recurring charge is to "keep a sadhana journal — inner and outer — and self-evaluate the state of each kosha daily, weekly and monthly," using whatever model works for you: "a car, a table, a bridge." [1], [4] See also: *Sankalpa*.

**Saha Navavatu** — *Sanskrit.* A peace mantra used *within* Shanti Path (see below), often chanted after OM, carrying "the intention that there is no distinction between student and teacher — we learn from each other and from the *vidya* itself, with openness, positivity, and non-hostility." [2], [3], [4] See also: *Shanti Path*, *OM*, *Vidya*.

**Sankalpa** — *Sanskrit.* A resolve or intention planted "in the quieter states after settling," where "we reach the subconscious." Fully done, it is more than words: "the affirmation, the waking-up of its emotional feeling, and its visualization." [3], [4] Perfecting the art of sankalpa — "what is it that I really, really want to experience, at its seed?" — is itself the practice of the causal body. See also: *Manomaya Kosha*, *Anandamaya Kosha*.

**Shanti Path (Shantipat)** — *Practice / method.* The deliberate creation of a space of peace, calm and receptivity — held both **before and after** a contained practice, and a practice in its own right. It settles the practitioner (witnessing, breath, the inner flame at the eyebrow or heart centre) so that whatever follows can truly land, and closes the practice by returning to that same stillness. A peace mantra such as *Saha Navavatu* or *OM* is one *tool* used within it — not the thing itself. [2], [3], [4] See also: *Saha Navavatu*, *OM*.

**Shatkarma** — *Sanskrit.* The Hatha-Yoga cleansing practices — Neti, Kunjal, Shankhaprakshalana and simple fasting — that "let us touch the autonomic systems" and reveal "how much food and cleansing influence body and mind." [3] See also: *Hatha Yoga*, *Laghoo Shankhaprakshalana*.

**Surya Namaskar** — *Sanskrit.* Sun Salutation; a dynamic sequence used to move prana — done "for several rounds, then be completely still and feel the nadis flowing through the body, major and minor." [2] See also: *Asana*, *Nadi*.

**SWAN** — A self-inquiry tool of Jnana Yoga: **S**trengths, **W**eaknesses, **A**mbitions, **N**eeds. "Constantly reviewing what this 'person called me' has accumulated… for the body, the mind, relationships, work." [3]

## T

**Tantra** — *Sanskrit.* Here defined broadly: "anything that expands consciousness and liberates energy." Mantra is named "a tool of Tantra," older than yoga — "it comes from a time of tantric discoveries." [4] See also: *Mantra*.

## V

**Vidya** — *Sanskrit.* Knowledge or science; "yoga vidya" is the whole interconnected body of yoga. The teacher's stated aim is "to model the interconnectedness of the koshas to the entire yoga vidya," grounding theory in practices "we can bring into daily life." [2], [3]

**Vijnanamaya Kosha** (विज्ञानमय) — *Sanskrit.* The fourth, wisdom or intuitive sheath (*vijnana* = deep knowing). "The sphere not only of all knowledge we have accumulated, but anything that can be known or conceived" — the space of Einstein's "leaps" and of the Akashic records. [1], [2] Its modern lens is "pattern recognition"; it is cultivated "by doing it," by "taking the larger perspective," and by listening for "your future self… talking to you right now." [3] See also: *Kosha*, *Akasha / Akashic records*.

## Y

**Yoga Nidra** — *Sanskrit.* "Yogic sleep"; a systematic practice of Shavasana, rotation of consciousness through the body, sound awareness, *sankalpa* and a backward breath-count. Also invoked as an image of wakefulness — "when you're sleeping, you know that you're sleeping." [2], [4] See also: *Sankalpa*, *Antarmouna*.
