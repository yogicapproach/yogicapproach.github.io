## Elements, Indriyas, and Why Sound Pervades [12:39]

One of the ideas that we had left with last time is this idea of sound and mantras and their influence on the *koshas*. According to yoga philosophy and metaphysics, the elements have different densities. And each of them has a corresponding sensory pathway or sense organ, which we call *indriyas*. Earth is considered the heaviest, and it associates with smell. Water is less dense, and it corresponds to taste, like saliva and spit. Fire is considered lighter, or less dense than water, and corresponds to sight and light. Then comes air or wind, which corresponds to touch. Then we have space or ether, which corresponds to sound. And then finally, the subtlest is the mind. Because sound is one of the subtlest sensory pathways, it's considered to pervade the others.

And we leverage that through practices which are very old. They predate yoga. They come from a time of tantric discoveries. So *mantra* is a tool of *tantra*, which has been adopted by yoga, which has been adopted by various religions. By *tantra*, I mean anything that expands the consciousness and liberates energy — that is the definition, loosely. And one of the practices that we'll do today is a short *Yoga Nidra*, which also utilizes sound. It allows the practitioner to be completely still and guided by external or internal sound in advanced stages.

## The Three Morning Mantras: The Waking Window and the Sankalpa [17:14]

Last time y'all heard three mantras recited just a few times each. In the tradition that I have done most of my training with, the *Satyananda* Yoga tradition, these three mantras are done instantly upon waking. And the reason for that is at the time of waking, we still have contact with our subconscious. And I'm sure you've experienced this: when you wake up, you still have access to dreams. Usually, as soon as we start to move, those dreams start to slip away. Or if we begin to talk or to think or interact with the outside world, they move away faster. And that phenomenon we can spend a lot of time observing, because it's incredible to me that you could have been a superhero or in a nightmare seconds before, but after awakening and moving, that entire world and experience can be totally inaccessible, to the point where you don't remember a single detail.

And yet that layer of the mind, the subconscious mind — ancient and modern psychology says — could be influencing 70% or more of our waking life. And so we believe, and we may have experienced, that subconscious impressions influence our waking life. Which is to say they influence all four *koshas*, and maybe all five. So how can we start to create a positive feedback loop that implants positive impressions into the subconscious mind while it's still accessible? One of the first ways is to put in positive affirmations right upon waking. And then to reinforce that positive seed throughout the day or week through the cultivation of yogic practices and lifestyle. So we'll be doing that with the full version of these three morning mantras.

And in a group this large, I understand that these mantras may be new — even the idea may not resonate. Wonderful. I want to emphasize today the setup of the mantras: the *sankalpa* or the affirmation, and in particular the waking up of the emotional feelings of that *sankalpa* and the visualization of that *sankalpa*. Both these skills are prerequisites in the higher branches of yoga, such as *bhakti yoga* and certainly *kriya yoga*. So as we chant these mantras, whether you do it out loud or just listen, keep bringing the mind back to the affirmation for the mantra. For example, the *Mahamrityunjaya* mantra is an ancient mantra for total well-being and protection. What does that look like to you? What does that actually feel like to you? When is the last time you felt great in all layers of your being? Was it when you were five years old and could jump around like a spring from couch to couch? Was it when you were a young adult, fully in your body, and you felt that your mind was sharp, and you were confident, and you felt safe in the world? Simultaneously, if there is a present-day condition that keeps you from feeling whole, can you imagine that part of you that could use healing in its healed state, in its state of well-being?

So in those first moments before the mantra, I really want you to put in that energy, that capacity of the mind to imagine and feel what it's imagining, because it can take years, decades, to awaken that capacity to its full potential.

From the yogic perspective, we would like to be able to feel cold when we think cold. To start sweating when we think hot. To feel heavy when we think heavy, and to feel light when we think light — and this goes for any example we can imagine. We already do this: if we see something sensual, usually there are signs in our body that we're starting to feel sensual. But we want to be able to stretch and apply this ability to feel per what we're thinking to anything that we choose at will, with the idea that we direct it towards our positive growth. And the last thing I'll say about this is: look into the book *The Brain That Changes Itself*, by — I think it's Norman Doidge. It is amazing modern-day case studies that validate ancient claims, such as that visualization can actually change the body.

For the final mantra, we bring our awareness down to the heart center at the sternum, connecting to the causal body. Perfecting the art of making *sankalpas* is a practice of the causal body, of *Anandamaya Kosha*, because it requires this patient revision of what it is that I really want to experience — really, really, at its seed. Underneath the words, underneath the conditioning, what is the deepest part of myself really asking to experience? I equate the mantras of *Durga* to the causal body, because she represents the causal force from which all of this manifest reality as we know it, including consciousness, comes.

Some religions depict her as a goddess, but there's no need to, unless that comes naturally. By what power has any of this come to be? Connect with that for *Durga*.

The *sankalpa*, the affirmation, is to first connect with the peace and harmony already present in our life — to see it, to recognize it, to trace back to the source of peace and harmony in our life. ## Practice: The Five Asanas (Done Dry) [45:56]

> *PRACTICE — A complete five-asana sequence done dry: the movements of Laghoo Shankhaprakshalana*

We're going to go through five asanas now — five amazing asanas. One thing that makes this asana sequence wonderful is that it is complete. There's spinal extension and lateral bending, twisting, forward bending, and backward bending. Additionally, it massages the digestive organs and requires deep breathing for maximum benefits. And so for any of us, when we're in a period of limited time, to address the needs of our entire physical body — including the respiratory and circulatory system — this sequence really addresses that. When done with current consumption of water, salt water in particular, this sequence will turn your body into a laundry machine and help push that water from mouth to anus, giving the entire digestive system a gentle wash.

This is the cleanse that is done every year, certainly by Janardhan and certain classes he sponsors, but also during the Shivaratri retreats. I want to do them with you now because it brings awareness to the *annamaya kosha*. The breathing and the exertion bring awareness to the *pranamaya kosha*. The coordination of movements and the awareness of what's being done, including the counting, awaken and bring awareness to *manomaya kosha*. And the sequence as a whole allows us to consider how yogic sequences are designed in larger patterns, to touch multiple layers at the same time. So they're really genius. They're never just what appears on the surface — physical exercises. So I invite you to do them; I will place my laptop to maybe demonstrate a few. We're only going to do five to eight rounds of each.

Start in a standing position. First one: feet together, hands interlaced, palms turned upwards on the head. Awareness in the spine, stretch long, coming to balance on the balls of the feet. And then come back onto the flats of the feet, hands returning to the top of the head. Inhale, long stretch up and balance. Exhale, return. Deep breath in, three. Exhale completely, down, three. Two more rounds on your own.

When you finish, relax the arms down by the sides, separate the feet about shoulder width apart, parallel. Once again, interlace the hands, palms up — actually, palms behind the back, elbows out to the sides. Bend slightly to the right, keeping the hips and feet fixed in space. Back up to center and smoothly to the left. Inhale to center, exhale right. Inhale center, exhale left. Awareness in the spine. Two more rounds on your own, awareness of the physical body, synchronizing movements with the breath. Notice what you feel, where you feel it, the intensity of the sensations.

When finished, relax the arms, keep the feet where they are, slight bend in the knees. Arms out to the side. Twist to the right, right hand behind the back, left hand pushing on the right shoulder. Return to center, and twist to the left, left hand behind the back, right hand pushing on the left shoulder. Full breath in to center, exhale, twist right. Full breath in, center, exhale, twist left. Repeat three more rounds, breath and movement synchronized. Feel the fingers moving through space. Feel the twist in the spine.

Imagine that you're watching yourself, observing yourself. For the next practice, you come down to lie on the floor in preparation for the cobra pose. The feet are a meter apart with the toes curled under, arms by the sides of the chest as if getting ready to do a push-up. The pubic bone remains on the ground through the whole practice. Lift the upper chest off the ground and twist left. Back through the center, twist right. Back to center, return to the floor. Exhale completely, inhale as you come up and hold the breath. Holding the breath, twist left, twist right, back to center, exhale down. Inhale up and hold, twist left, twist right. Exhaling down once you've returned to center. Two more rounds on your own. Where do you feel the practice? In what ways does it feel familiar or new? What is the experience on the spine, on the internal organs? How does it challenge or work with your breath?

And when finished, come into a squatting position, if okay for the knees. A simple spinal twist lying on the back is an okay alternative, but we will not go into it today. If a squatting position is suitable for you, you can place the hands on the knees. Shift the weight to the left foot and allow the right knee to come towards the floor. Push the left knee into the body and use that to twist to the left. Come back to center. Shift the weight to the right foot, and let the left knee come towards the floor. Push the right knee into the body as you twist right. Inhale, center. Exhale, twist left. Inhale, center, exhale, twist right. Up to three more rounds on your own.

This is a strong practice on the knees, hips, and spine, so be gentle. And when finished, come to lie in *Shavasana*, maybe with the head facing the laptop or computer and the feet away, so the ears are closest to the speakers.

## Practice: Yoga Nidra [1:01:32]

> *PRACTICE — Yoga Nidra: stillness, sound awareness, sankalpa, and rotation of consciousness*

Ensure that you will be able to remain warm for the next 10 to 15 minutes. The legs are hip width apart, wide enough that the sacrum and the backs of the buttocks can really settle into the floor. Legs rolling outwards as comfortable for the hips. Palms turned upwards and far enough apart from the body that the shoulder blades and shoulders can settle to the floor. The back of the head balanced along the midline. As soon as you're able, find a stillness within the position. If you are sitting, you can still practice with the eyes closed, moving the awareness as directed.

Feel into the whole physical body as it is right now — the legs, the arms, the torso, neck, and head. The bones, the muscles, fluids, organs. The points of contact between your body and the floor. Really find and feel those exact points of contact between body and floor — along the back of the legs, contact points along the back of the arms, the back of the torso, neck, and head, between the whole body and the floor. Mentally tell yourself to let go of your body. Release the weight of your whole body onto the earth below, and release any management of the breath so that it too becomes natural and spontaneous. And as the body, breath, and mind settle on their own, take the awareness into the space around you. Imagining yourself to be a sensitive radar, note any sound you hear. Hearing it, disconnect, find another sound. Hearing another sound, let it go, find another — keep the mind active, moving from sound to sound.

Listen for the faintest sound that can be heard in the distance, and hearing anything, find another faint sound.

Bring that awareness of hearing into your own body and listen for internal sounds — the heart, the breath, the nervous system.

Listen and feel for the silence between these rhythmic patterns. Between two heartbeats, there is stillness and silence; between two breaths, there is stillness and silence. Wait for that moment and observe it.

This is the time to repeat your *sankalpa*, a positive idea that represents a deep transformation you wish to bring to yourself. If you're not sure what that is, you can make the *sankalpa*: I intuit my true *sankalpa*. I become aware of my deepest life calling, or soul desire.

The awareness will now be taken on a journey through the body. Hearing the name of a body part, touch the mind to that area. There is no need to move any body part — only awareness to that point. Right hand. Wrist. Forearm. Elbow. Upper arm. Shoulder. Armpit, under the arm. Right side of the torso. Right hip. Right foot. Awareness in the left hand. Wrist. Forearm. Elbow. Upper arm. Armpit, or underarm. Left side of the torso. Left foot. Awareness in the back of the legs. Buttocks. Back of the torso. Back of the arms. Back of the neck and head. Feel the whole face. Front of the torso. Pelvic region. Arms and legs. Awareness of the whole right side of the body. The whole left side of the body. Awareness of the whole body. Experience the whole body from tiptoe to crown of head. Full body awareness.

Within the stillness of the physical body, the natural breath has been flowing on its own. Find, feel, and observe the movements of the natural breath. With the awareness at the entrance of the nasal passages, feel each breath entering the body, awareness of each breath exiting the body. And count each natural breath backwards from five to one. For example: five, awareness of breathing in; five, awareness of breathing out; four, awareness of breathing in — until you reach zero. Wherever you are in that count, let it go.

## Practice: Antarmouna and Externalizing [1:14:41]

> *PRACTICE — Antarmouna: witnessing the mind at chidakash, then externalizing back through the koshas*

Be aware of any activity or non-activity in the mind. Look into that mental screen of *chidakash* behind the forehead and closed eyes. Whatever may appear there, be the observer, the witness.

Imagine the lightness of the air that surrounds the physical body. Feel the heaviness of the body lying on the floor. Begin to inhale more intentionally, deeper in both directions, gradually reconnecting with the sounds around you. With each deepening breath, the smells, the temperature in the room. Small movements in the fingers and toes, the wrists and ankles. You can give yourself a long stretch as if waking up in bed in the morning — a good squeeze of the knees to the chest, and then rocking side to side, eventually coming up into a comfortable sitting position. When you come up, keep the eyes closed and the body still for a few moments, just noticing any effects of this short relaxation. Rotate your awareness through the five *koshas*.

And then do a round of palming to complete the externalization, lowering the hands when you're ready.

## The Table of the Five Koshas [1:18:11]

I'll close with the final example that has worked really well for me in life in the past few years. This AI image made a very weird table, and since I tend to make my slides last minute, I have not updated it. But I want you to imagine there are only four legs, like a normal table. I had an experience in the last few years of life where I was physically and energetically at my peak, even mentally — but emotionally, I had been ignoring things for a number of years. You can imagine that a table, even with three legs, still does balance. A table with three legs will balance until you knock out another leg, or you put something on the corner of the table that doesn't have a leg underneath it.

And so it happened in my life — which was a blessing — that a few things did happen at the same time that both shook the other legs and put a lot of emotional weight on the one corner that didn't have it.

And the table, which represents myself, came crashing down. It was a blessing because it forced me to reevaluate all aspects of my life, and which parts of myself I'd been deeply ignoring that would create such a collapse. During that time, I started to connect with a number of private students who were sort of going through their own life collapse. And so it was an interesting opportunity to test many aspects of yoga in a real way. And now it's part of my daily, weekly, and certainly monthly self-evaluation: what is the condition of each of these legs? The first four *koshas* — *annamaya*, *pranamaya*, *manomaya*, and *vijnanamaya* — represent the four legs, and *anandamaya* is the balance, the stability of the whole table. I'm still finding this metaphor works for me. For example, I see how, due to my own ambitiousness and conditioning, I try and make these legs very, very long. I want to be super strong and fit and attractive. I want to be able to work all day, sleep as little as possible. When I do sleep, I want to have lucid dreams and visit gurus of my past lives. I don't want to forget anything. I want to be able to manage multiple projects and read some number of books a year while learning multiple languages. And to be intuitive enough to predict the future, and empathetic enough to know how all my loved ones feel and what they need. And this vision of myself is great, until one thing needs to be taken from the top of the table, or the table shakes and anything on top falls — because when they do fall, they fall a really long way.

The opposite of that could be to cultivate a lot of simplicity and humility and to build a stable table with very short legs — but also risk other people using my table as a stepping stool. I do use an internal and external journal to see where the legs of this table are. And I recommend that you find a system that works for you to really connect with the state of each of your *koshas*, and to find a model that really works for you, whether it's a car, a table, a bridge, whatever.
